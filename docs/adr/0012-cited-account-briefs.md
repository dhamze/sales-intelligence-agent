# ADR-0012: Cited account briefs and how they are evaluated

- **Status:** Accepted
- **Date:** 2026-11-04

## Context

Sellers want a short brief per account and a suggested way to open the conversation. Free-form
generated text is where language models are most likely to state things the sources never
said, and a brief is only useful if a seller can trust it without re-checking everything.

## Decision

1. **Closed book, numbered sources.** The model sees only what we already hold: website pages
   read during enrichment (W1, W2, ...; at most 60,000 characters), the company record (R), the
   ICP fit and its breakdown (S) and contacts (C). No web access, no tools.
2. **Contacts by job title only** (decision 2026-11-04). Names, emails and phone numbers are
   never sent to the model; titles are enough to tailor an angle.
3. **Every claim is cited and checked.** The schema requires a source ID and a verbatim quote
   per citation. `verify()` keeps a citation only if the quote (normalised for case, spacing,
   quote and dash styles; at least 8 characters) occurs in the cited source, and a claim only if
   one citation survives. The outreach angle needs verified evidence too. Dropped claims are
   counted and shown ("2 unverifiable claims removed").
4. **Inferences are labelled.** "Likely priorities" are the model's inferences, shown as such,
   and still must cite the facts they rest on.
5. **Out-of-date detection.** A SHA-256 fingerprint of all sources (and the prompt version) is
   stored with the brief; when anything changes (re-enrichment, an edit, a new score, a
   contact) the brief is marked out of date. Briefs are never regenerated automatically.
6. **Cost control.** Generated by the worker on request only; every call (including failures)
   is logged with its estimated cost; 50 briefs per organisation per day; Opus 5.5 at medium
   effort by default (`SIA_BRIEF_MODEL`, `SIA_BRIEF_EFFORT`), revisited after the live eval.
7. **Evaluation without a judge model.** `tests/evals` holds 12 fixed, fictional cases (10
   companies, one never enriched, one prompt-injection page). Grading is mechanical: citation
   coverage (target >= 90%, from the product brief), required topics, forbidden content, and
   admitting gaps on thin data. The harness runs in CI with the fake model; the live comparison
   runs only on demand (`--run-live-llm`).

## Alternatives considered

- **LLM-as-judge grading:** catches subtler problems (tone, usefulness), but costs money per
  run, varies between runs and needs its own validation. Possible later on top of the
  mechanical checks.
- **Letting the model browse:** fresher information, but unverifiable claims and a much larger
  injection surface.
- **Automatic regeneration on change:** spends money on accounts nobody is looking at.

## Consequences

- A brief can be thinner than a free-writing model would produce; that is the intended trade.
- Verification proves a quote exists in a source, not that the claim's wording follows from
  it. The eval's relevance and forbidden-content checks, and the UI showing each quote next to
  its claim, cover that gap.
